Project Megenagna (መገናኛ — "junction") is a 4-layer, offline-first digital addressing grid for Ethiopia — open standard, no licensing fees, designed for 3G/2G reality. It's early: Milestone 1 hasn't started. We need experts, not just code.

Problem & Incentive

Why this matters, and to whom

No structured, nationwide pinpoint addressing system means every delivery, dispatch, and civil process in Ethiopia runs on phone calls and shared pins. That's not a UX gap — it's a standing tax on the economy.

1

The "phone-call-first" delivery model

Couriers (Deliver Addis, BeU) and ride-hailing drivers (RIDE, Feres, Yango) call customers repeatedly or exchange Telegram/WhatsApp pins for every trip — friction, delay, and telecom cost on every transaction.

2

Emergency response delays

Ambulances, fire engines, and police dispatchers lose critical minutes navigating by informal landmarks instead of a precise, callable address.

3

Financial inclusion & civil administration

Proving physical residence for banking credit scoring, postal delivery, tax audits, and civil process serving is extremely difficult without a verifiable address.

4

Traditional numbering can't be waited for

Western sequential house numbering took 200+ years of heavy municipal infrastructure to build. Emerging markets need a digital leapfrog — not a multi-decade street-naming campaign.

Research

How the world has tried this — and what actually worked

Digital addressing has been attempted before. The pattern is consistent: open standards with government enforcement succeed; proprietary or unenforced systems stall.

What worked

Dubai, UAE — Makani

10-digit geo-coordinate per building entrance, physically installed by government mandate and integrated into taxi apps and dispatch. Top-down enforcement was the decisive factor.

India — DIGIPIN

Open, non-proprietary 4m × 4m national grid, directly linked to Aadhaar digital identity and banking. Open standard + national ID integration drove adoption.

Kenya & Nigeria — OkHi

Smartphone-anchored verification: GPS pin + door photo + phone number. Private-sector e-commerce adoption without waiting for government rollout.

What didn't

Ghana — GhanaPostGPS

5m × 5m grid on proprietary underlying software, poor public education, no integration with land registries. Adoption stalled.

what3words (for civic use)

Proprietary risk — governments locked into a private licensing model — and no spatial logic: adjacent locations get unrelated word triples, unlike postal codes where digits encode hierarchy.

The lesson we're designing against

Open standard (like DIGIPIN) + enforcement mechanism (like Makani) + real hierarchy (unlike what3words) is the combination worth copying. See Layer 3 below for where enforcement is still an open risk for us too.

Current Proposal

The 4-Layer Open Hybrid Model

Each layer is independently useful and works offline; together they bridge a raw GPS grid to a legally binding, physically discoverable address.

LAYER 1
Open-Standard Digital GridOpen Location Code (Plus Codes) — royalty-free, zero-dependency, 100% offline encode/decode. ~14m × 14m resolution (10-char), ~3m × 3m sub-door (11-char).
LAYER 2
Administrative Hierarchy & Fayda BindingJurisdiction-aware address hierarchy, bridging grid codes to Ethiopia's Fayda Biometric National ID for KYC and residency verification.
LAYER 3
Physical QR-Coded Door PlaquesBilingual (English / Amharic Ge'ez) high-contrast plates with offline-scannable QR codes that open exact navigation.
LAYER 4
Open APIs, Backend & Cross-Platform AppsREST/OpenAPI + PostGIS backend, Flutter mobile apps, a lightweight PWA, and a batch-geocoding CLI.

Worked examples — the hierarchy is jurisdiction-aware, not fixed

Addis Ababa's Woreda absorbed Kebele's role in a 2011 E.C. restructuring; Kebele remains the lowest unit everywhere else in the country, including Dire Dawa.

ET-AA-LK-W03-H104-8FW4+9X ET-DD-W01-K05-8FW4+9X

Country → Region → Sub-City/Zone → Woreda → [Kebele] → House → Grid Code. Format is currently DRAFT v0.1 — see Open Questions below.

Where your specific expertise plugs in

Not "we need developers" — each card below ties to a real, named gap in the current plan. Pick the one you actually know something about.

🗺️

GIS & Open-Data Researchers

Help source an authoritative, licensable gazetteer of Ethiopia's region/zone/woreda/kebele boundaries and names — the missing piece blocking Milestone 2.

⚖️

Policy & Legal / Data Protection

Fayda ID access under Proclamation No. 1321/2024 is the project's single largest external dependency. Institutional and legal expertise here is critical-path.

🏙️

Urban Planners & Municipal Liaisons

Physical plaque rollout needs a funded, enforced installation path — the exact failure mode GhanaPostGPS hit. Help us not repeat it.

🧮

TypeScript / Algorithm Engineers

Milestone 1 needs a zero-dependency, strictly-typed port of the Open Location Code algorithm plus an ET-NAS address parser, tested against Google's own fixtures.

🗄️

Backend / PostGIS Engineers

Milestone 2's shared backend needs RLS-secured, offline-sync-aware API design — conflict resolution is spec'd, implementation isn't.

📱

Mobile Engineers (Flutter/Kotlin)

Milestone 4's offline camera QR scanning and cached vector-tile navigation for low-end Android devices — real constraints, not a demo app.

🚚

Logistics & Ride-Hailing Partnerships

🔤

Localization & Ge'ez Typography

Print-safe vector fonts for physical plaques and professional translation across Amharic, Tigrinya, Afaan Oromoo, and Somali.

🔐

Security Reviewers
